About the project

About ClipCheck Studio

A small, free set of checks for people who make vertical videos. It helps you catch framing, file and subtitle problems before a clip reaches an audience.

Why this toolkit exists

Most creators edit on a desktop or in a phone app, then publish to a feed that recomposes the result. A clip that looks fine in the timeline can arrive with a subject hidden behind a button, captions buried under a profile name, or a subtitle file that feels rushed. Those problems are boring to diagnose but expensive to fix after publishing.

ClipCheck Studio was built to make that diagnosis quick. It does not try to replace a video editor or a platform's own upload checks. Instead, it answers a narrow set of practical questions: is the frame tall enough, is the resolution sensible, where might interfaces cover the image, and can the subtitles be read at the speed they appear?

What you can do here

  • Inspect a local file. Open a video or image and see its dimensions, simplified ratio, duration, size and media type.
  • Preview approximate safe areas. Switch between common vertical targets to see where controls and captions may sit.
  • Export a transparency guide. Download a PNG with only the risky zones painted, ready to place above your timeline.
  • Check SRT captions. Find timestamp conflicts, excessive reading speed, long lines and crowded cues.

How the tools are built

The entire site is plain HTML, CSS and vanilla JavaScript. There is no build step, paid service, database or server component. Your browser reads the file you choose and performs the calculations locally. That keeps the tool free to host, simple to audit and usable without an account.

The safe-area percentages are deliberately approximate. They reflect common layouts for tall feeds rather than a published specification from any company. Treating them as planning guides avoids a false sense of precision: a phone model, app update or account setting can move interface elements by a noticeable amount.

What ClipCheck Studio is not

It is not a video converter, compressor, subtitle translator or publishing service. It does not guarantee that a platform will accept a file, and it does not promise a particular reach or ranking. It is also not connected to the companies whose platform names appear as convenient labels. See the privacy policy for how data is handled, or get in touch with a suggestion.

A note on accessibility

Controls use real labels, visible focus states and live result regions so the tools work with a keyboard and screen reader. The layout starts narrow and expands on larger screens, and motion is reduced when your system asks for it. If something is hard to use, that is a bug worth reporting.
